Theoretical Insights: Why Cosine Similarity?In content-based recommendation systems,
 text is converted into high-dimensional vectors (such as via TF-IDF). 
 Cosine Similarity is used to calculate the directional closeness between these vectors rather than calculating raw Euclidean distance.Mathematically,
  it measures the cosine of the angle between two multi-dimensional vectors in a vector space.
  Key benefits for NLP and Recommendations:Length Independence: 
  Euclidean distance scales poorly if one description is very short and another is highly verbose, 
  mistakenly making them look distant. Cosine similarity evaluates the normalized vector angle, 
  meaning it focuses entirely on the relative keyword composition and semantic profile rather than text length.
  Bounded Scale: The output is bound neatly between $0$ (completely orthogonal, meaning zero matching terms) and $1$ (identical relative word distribution), 
  making threshold tuning highly predictable.
